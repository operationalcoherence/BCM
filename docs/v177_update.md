# v177 (preview only) — 2026-11-17

Priority 2, T1–T3: Taking Action's ways forward are compared. All new wording is Claude's and is marked for his pass.

On every strategy card with two or three ways (A, B and sometimes C, switched on and off with the Show buttons), THE WAYS FORWARD, SKETCHED ON THE GROUND now adds:

- **"X: why choose it"** for each way, read from the ground:
  - the **harder way**: most energy, but it meets the most notes held in distrust, aggression or defiance, in the most emotions. Choose it when the goal cannot be reached around those people, only with them.
  - the **way that saves energy**: least climbing, on ground that holds, passing acceptance. Choose it to build momentum first, as Vigneaux (2026) puts it, with the harder ground after.
  - a way that meets resistance **without the steepest climb**, or a **middle way**, when there is one.
- **Why take the harder way?** Going around the resistance leaves it as it was. The harder way is how the ground itself changes. A common order is both: the easy way first, small, then the harder way.
- **When to stop and readjust**, built on Morin's ecology of action (Morin, 2008, p. 55). Watch the indifference notes the ways pass (they can tip either way), stuck places, and "programs" that need constant attention. Then return to the map and read the ground again. Strategy vs. program (Morin, 2008).
- **COMPARE THE WAYS IN A CHART** (expandable): bars for Effort, Impact, Benefit and Return on effort for each way, in the way's own color, relative to the largest on that card.

How the numbers are made (route stats, `routeStats` in the terrain module):
- effort = height climbed along the way + 0.15 × its length relative to a straight run;
- impact = notes in distrust, aggression or defiance within reach of the way + ½ × the number of emotions met;
- benefit = acceptance notes met + ½ × indifference notes met;
- return on effort = (impact + benefit) / effort.
"Within reach" = 0.8 × the map's note spacing (sigma). These are relative to his own map and are not a standard measure.
