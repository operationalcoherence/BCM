## 0. BCM Core Structure (foundational architecture)
**Foundational definition [D 11-18 19:52; promoted to first-class architecture 11-18 19:58].** This is architecture, not commentary. Every other section of this document serves it, and §0.9 binds all future work to it.

### 0.1 The deeper question [D 11-18 20:05]
> **What holds this situation where it is, and which transitions toward the goal are possible, through which emotions, and at what cost?**

Short form, for headings and the screen: **What holds it, and where can it give?**

The method asks about the **situation**. The ground is how BCM draws it (§0.5). The second half is the job Vigneaux (2026) gives the possibility landscape: which transitions are needed, through which emotions, how much energy to spend, and where. "Cost" means energy, the paper's term; "climb" is its terrain word. Every page answers part of the question.

### 0.1a Foundational and representational concepts [D 11-18 20:05]
**Rule:** foundational definitions are never written in terrain terms. Terrain words describe how a foundation is shown, never what it is.

**Foundational** (from the paper or the seven questions; they hold however the method draws them):

| Concept | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **The goal** | The idea or practice that is meant to take root | Q.1, Q.4; the paper's "resilience-building idea" |
| **Disposition** | Emotion as a bodily disposition for action: it sets which actions are possible for a person now. The five emotions run from defiance to acceptance by how open they are to a new idea | Maturana; Vigneaux (2026, pp. 26–28) |
| **Possibility** | What can take hold now, given people's dispositions (the paper's "spatial possibilities") | Vigneaux (2026, p. 29) |
| **Conditions** | What holds dispositions and habits in place, or lets them change: context, environment, language, institutions, habits, and self-feeding loops | Q.1, Q.2, Q.4; the environment and language strategies; Juarrero's constraints |
| **Transition** | A change of disposition. Each has an energy cost; most go through indifference; none is stable, acceptance included | Vigneaux (2026, p. 27) |
| **Energy** | The cost of a transition | Vigneaux (2026, p. 27) |
| **Habit** (already doing something) | Self-maintaining patterns people already live in; support that already exists starts here | Sensorimotor schemes; Vigneaux (2026, p. 30) |
| **Perceiver dependence** | What counts as the relevant world depends on who perceives it; voices read the same thing differently | Varela |
| **Invitation, not instruction** | Action perturbs; it can't dictate. Design changes conditions (environment, language) or works through dispositions (emotions-first, behaviors-first) | Vigneaux (2026, pp. 29–30) |
| **Change over time** | Nothing is stable, so the method maps, acts, and maps again | §1 [D] |

**Representational** (how BCM shows the foundations; a visual language, not the subject):

| Representation | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **The ground / terrain / relief** | The whole possibility landscape |
| **Height** (high ground; low ground: distrust low, aggression lower, defiance lowest) | Disposition: how open people are to the goal |
| **The ridge** | Indifference, the emotion of transition |
| **Bare rock, a climb** | A high-energy transition between distant emotions with no indifference between them |
| **Slopes, falls, hollows** | Which way a transition tends to run; conditions that keep people low |
| **The help and hinder line** | Whether a condition holds the goal back or lets it move |
| **Present toward the goal** (left to right) | Time within one picture (now and later) |
| **Colors** (Starratt's palette) | The five dispositions |
| **The possibility landscape** | The paper's name for the finished visualization |
| **Lines, threads, ways A/B/C, the tour** | Relations, readings, and moves drawn on the representation |

"Ground" has one narrow foundational sense, from the paper: **fertile ground**, acceptance as where the goal can take root (Q.1 and Q.4 say "take root"). Used for the whole map, "ground" is representational.

### 0.2 The four core questions
Each is defined by the foundations (§0.1a). Its terrain representation comes second.

1. **What holds it?** Which **conditions** keep present dispositions and habits where they are: holding, resisting, hindering, and conditions that feed themselves (the loop).
   *Drawn as:* low ground on the hindering side, hollows, the loop.
2. **What already moves toward the goal?** Where the goal is **already possible**: dispositions that allow it, and behaviors already under way (enabling, support, the opportunity).
   *Drawn as:* high ground on the helping side.
3. **Where can it shift, and at what cost?** Which **transitions** are possible, through which emotions, and with how much **energy**. Big moves between distant emotions take energy either way; most transitions go through indifference.
   *Drawn as:* the ridge, bare rock, the climb.
4. **Where does the reading split?** Where **perceivers** read the conditions differently. It has two faces:
   - *the situation splits:* a word pulled two ways, or two voices on two sides;
   - *the picture splits:* what was said against what was mapped, a note's place against its ties, or a reading that rests on little.

   *Drawn as:* one thing standing in two places at once.

### 0.3 The central test
> **Does what you would design change the conditions and invite the transitions the goal needs?**

Short form, for the screen: **Does what you would design meet the ground?**

This is the seam between **the context you have grasped** (Q.1–Q.4) and **what you would design** (Q.5–Q.7). It isn't a fifth question. It is the test that holds the second half against the first, and it follows the paper's strategies: change the environment and language (conditions), and work through emotions or behaviors (transitions). Design has to meet:
- what holds it (otherwise the hold is left unanswered);
- what already moves (otherwise support is left unbuilt-on);
- where it can shift (otherwise the conditions for a transition go unmet).

### 0.4 The seven questions are the core questions, asked as input
The reading pages answer the seven questions again, one level up, as structure instead of answers.

| Core question | Fed by |
|---|---|
| What holds it? | Q.1 (why the goal hasn't taken root), Q.2 (opposition) |
| What already moves toward the goal? | Q.3 (support), Q.7 (reinforce) |
| Where can it shift, and at what cost? | Q.4 (conditions to establish), Q.6 (redesign) |
| Where does the reading split? | Every question, read against the map and across voices |
| The central test | Q.5–Q.7 held against Q.1–Q.4 |

### 0.5 Terrain is the representation
Terrain is BCM's visual language for the foundations (§0.1a). It is not their definition, and the core questions would hold for a place map, for text, or for any future view.

| Foundation | Terrain |
|---|---|
| Conditions that hold | Low ground on the hindering side, hollows, the loop |
| Possibility (where the goal can already take hold) | High ground on the helping side |
| Transitions and their energy | Slopes, the ridge (indifference), bare rock, the climb |
| Disposition | Height: acceptance high, indifference a ridge, distrust low, aggression lower, defiance lowest |

Each core question reads one part of the situation; the terrain shows where. The central test asks whether what you would design lands there.

### 0.6 How each page contributes

| Page | Its question | What only it does | What would be lost without it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pattern Analysis** | **Why does it hold?** (mainly question 1, with 2 and 3) | The explanation in words, built from the user's own language: YOUR STORY SO FAR and WHERE THIS LEAVES THE WORK, then WHY IT BEHAVES THIS WAY (the system that holds, the key tension, the loop, the opportunity, and where it can cross) | The reasons. The ground would show where things hold but never why |
| **Pattern Synthesis** | **Where does it hold, and where can it give?** (questions 1–3 as position and cost) | Position and cost on the ground: high and low ground, what keeps it this way, where it's easiest to fall or get stuck, the ridge, bare rock, and the price of each crossing. The only page that measures what a move takes | "At what cost." Every move would look equally possible |
| **In Parallel** | **Where does the reading split?** (question 4) | Two independent readings checked against each other: the words (Analysis) and the ground (Synthesis). It finds hidden disagreements and agreements, emotional turns, overlaps in meaning, bridges, and hinges that neither page shows alone | The check. The picture would be taken as settled |
| **Taking Action** | **Does what you would design change the conditions and invite the transitions, and how would it move?** (the central test) | Turns the readings into ways (a program on ground that holds, a strategy that crosses low ground), costs each way, gives first moves, records the decision, and logs WHAT YOU DID, which closes the map → act → re-map loop | The consequence. Nothing would be decided or tried, and the loop could never close |

Mapping Context and Mapping Landscape gather the input: the goal, the seven answers, and the notes with their place, emotion, source, and voice.

### 0.7 Recurrence is by design
The same finding often appears on several pages. On the 11-18 workbook, "good fire" pulled two ways appears on all four pages, and so does tradition left unanswered. Each page answers the same core question from its own angle (words, ground, check, action). Repetition across pages is acceptable when the angle differs. Within one page it is not.

### 0.8 Time: a question already present, not yet read
**"What has shifted since last time?"** is the four core questions asked across saves: what still holds, what has started to move, what has shifted, and where the reading has changed.
- It's within scope: the living loop (map → act → re-map) is decided [D] (§1).
- Its data already exists:
  - emotion history per note (`eh`, v193);
  - WHAT YOU DID and the notes flagged to re-map (`acts`, `remap`, v194);
  - SINCE YOU ACTED on Mapping Landscape.
- No reading page reads it yet. The history is used only as a cost (hysteresis).

Within one save, the method reads the present against what lies toward the goal (now and later). Between saves, it doesn't yet read anything.

### 0.9 The governing rule [D 11-18 19:58]
All future BCM work maps back to the deeper question (§0.1), the four core questions (§0.2), and the central test (§0.3), where applicable. That covers:
- features;
- pages;
- visualizations;
- analyses (Pattern Analysis);
- syntheses (Pattern Synthesis, In Parallel);
- action pathways (Taking Action: programs, strategies, ways, first moves);
- multi-workbook capabilities (Combine, voices, F8 project lead dashboards, and reading change across saves).

Before anything is built, its design states:
1. **Which core question it answers** (1–4), or that it applies **the central test**, or that it serves the deeper question directly.
2. **Which conditions, possibilities, or transitions it reads**, and how terrain represents them (§0.5).
3. **What it adds that no existing page or section already gives**, and what would be lost without it (§0.6, §0.7).
4. **Which canonical terms it uses** (§0a).

Work that can't name a core question, the test, or the deeper question doesn't belong in the method until it can. Input steps (Mapping Context, Mapping Landscape) are exempt from item 2, but they state which core questions their input feeds (§0.4).

Multi-workbook capabilities read the same structure across people and across time: whose conditions hold or move and how each perceiver reads them (voices, F8), and what has shifted between saves (§0.8).

### 0a.1 Names that mean two things today
- **"Loop"** means a self-feeding cycle (THE LOOP) and also a drawn phrase pattern ("phrase loop").
- **"Reinforce"** means Q.7 (strengthen what works) and also "reinforce one another" (a self-supporting hold). These are opposite meanings.
- **"Bridge"** means a thread across the two halves of the questions, a note between emotions (PLACED ONE WAY), and the costed crossings of bare rock.
- **"Pattern"** is the engine's name ("Patterns suggests"), the drawn phrase patterns, POSSIBLE PATTERNS, and "Patterns worth a second look."
- **"High ground," "raised ground," and "firm ground"** are used as if they were the same thing.

## 0b. BCM Missing Questions
What the core structure needs and the pages don't yet fully ask (11-18). Identification only; nothing here is a feature request.

1. **Taking Action doesn't ask where the reading splits.** It acts on words that are pulled two ways and on the ground, but it doesn't carry forward what In Parallel found. When the same word means something different to two voices, In Parallel says a course of action "will be heard differently by each"; the ways on the cards don't hold that.
2. **Pattern Synthesis asks the central test only lightly** ("Not in Q.5–Q.7 yet," in WHAT IT MIGHT MEAN).
3. **"Whose ground is it?"** runs under all four questions but is never asked as a question of its own. Emotions belong to people, and Vigneaux (2026) orients practitioners to the people in each emotion. Voices exist, but BCM asks *where* far more than *who*. Today it shows up only as a dimension of the split (two voices on two sides, the thinnest voice).
4. **"What has shifted since last time?"** is a hidden question already present, not a missing page and not outside scope (see §0.8). It belongs to all four questions across saves.

## 1. Purpose
BCM is a free web workbook from Operational Coherence. It helps a practitioner, or later a group, work out how to move a goal forward in a complex social setting. It does this by reading the emotional and structural terrain around the goal.
- Users have read none of the source papers. The app must carry that theory for them. Vigneaux (2026) is the core text.
- Output rule: **tell users what they don't already know.** Give synthesis, relations and meta-meaning, never a recount of what they wrote.
- Presentation rule: **story over list.** Findings are written as narrative prose built from the user's own data points, and those data points keep their hover/tap popovers.
- Long-term direction [D]: BCM should be a living decision loop (map → act → re-map), not a one-off assessment.

## 5. Emotion model (Vigneaux, 2026; Starratt painting)
Export index → emotion. The mapping is confirmed by the note content and matches the painting's color order:

| idx | Emotion | Color | Adoption potential (1 = best) | Terrain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acceptance | teal | 1 | the highest ground |
| 3 | Indifference | yellow | 2 | **ridge**, the transitional crossing |
| 2 | Distrust | lavender | 3 | low ground |
| 0 | Aggression | red | 4 | lower ground |
| 4 | Defiance | deep ultramarine `#1b2a8f` (v171, matches the painting) | 5 | lowest ground |

Rules:
- Indifference is the emotion of transition. Most moves pass through it. It can also be a resting state.
- Transition cost is an energy gradient. Defiance→acceptance is rare and high-energy. Distrust↔acceptance costs more than indifference→acceptance.
- No emotion is stable, acceptance included.
- Strategies: **emotions-first** (for acceptance, indifference and transitional zones) and **behaviors-first** (for aggression, distrust and defiance: introduce the behavior without its label).

## 6. Terrain model (Pattern Synthesis 3D)
Legend [D]:
- Indifference = ridge · Distrust = low ground · Aggression = lower ground · Defiance = lowest ground.
- **Bare rock face**: two distant emotions meet with no indifference ridge between them. Crossing is a climb. Big moves cost energy; most moves go through indifference.
- Plain grass/soil/rock, no color = no notes nearby, so no information.
- Routes: **A** · its way through its notes; **B** · through its notes on firm ground only.
- Pins = notes in their emotion color · black flags = questions · present flag green · goal flag checkered (both since v171).
- Axes: ← PRESENT / FUTURE → along the front; ↑ HELP / ↓ HINDER back to front.
- **Indifference ridge must follow indifferent notes all the way**, even into another emotion's area [D]. (N31 currently adds color but no terrain.)
- Controls: drag to turn, scroll/pinch to zoom, hover/tap to inspect.
- Fullscreen must include the legend and the "The Bear Canyon Method · Operational Coherence" mark, with rotation and expansion [D].
- **Guided tours** (v208–v210, v219; in relief.js: startTour/stepTour/pauseTour/resumeTour/stopTour). Play opens full screen; one large Play/Pause at bottom center with End tour; captions 17/20 px held by reading time (3.3 words/s, 2.4–6 s a stop). Synthesis: READING THE GROUND (two stops, `D.intro` from `introStops(false)`), then every reading in WHAT THE GROUND SAYS. Analysis: a primer on the user's own notes (`introStops(true)`: high ground, ridge, bare rock, deepest ground, what a move costs). Captions never cite the paper [D 11-18].
- **Bare rock in the engine** (v210): `rockFaces()` replicates the relief's rock rule (two strongest emotions nearly balanced, ≥2 steps apart, neither indifference; indifference band included) to find the largest face for its reading.
- **Lines on the relief** (v213, v214): `hoses(spec)` draws a word's lines (thin black, or dashed red) from the In Parallel figure; `window.gsmSynRel`, `gsmSynHost`, and `gsmSynPend` (lines wait for the Synthesis 3D to mount).

### Terrain engine principles (adopted from the review)
- **Topological anchoring** — the goal, the PRESENT/FUTURE axes and the question flags are fixed anchors. A note edit triggers only local semantic relaxation, never a global re-layout, so the user's spatial memory survives.
- **Synthetic bridging** — a bare rock face is a design challenge, not a dead stop. The engine estimates the cost of building a transitional (indifference) bridge where no notes link the two sides.
- **Hysteresis** — crossing cost increases with the note's emotional lineage (entrenched vs. transient opposition).
- **Attractor basins** (Juarrero) — separate *trajectory optimization* (the cheapest path inside the current constraints) from *constraint reconfiguration* (changing the rules that make the basin). Ties to Q.4–Q.7.
- **Voice normalization** — weight voices so that a small but dense voice is not flattened by note count.

## 8. Threads and linking
- **Thread** [D]: a graph edge between notes/answers that share specific semantic tokens or are close in vector space, **across question boundaries (Q.1–Q.7)**. The engine today builds threads from shared words and two-word phrases. On screen: "a word or phrase that two or more answers or notes share", defined in place (v171) and in Start Here's glossary.
- **Pattern that runs through it** (Pattern Synthesis): a two-word phrase used in more than one note or answer, drawn as a loop on the 3D map when ticked.
- **Links**: user-made note↔note links, created on Mapping Landscape (`data.links`). These replace the old "what you linked" section, which belonged to the old figure.

## 9. Text engine
Current: stemmed unigrams and bigrams, a 352-entry stopword list, proper-noun handling, role-based scoring (v113+) ("liveli", "wildfi force", "putt fire"). Recurrence counts drive many findings.

Shipped (v172–v173):
- **Saturation filter** (v172): a stem in ≥40% of the pieces (goal, answers, notes; at least 10 pieces) that also heads ≥2 recurring bigrams is the medium. Its count is set to 1, so no finding, card or reading names the bare word; its phrases carry the meaning. `window.gsmMED` = {word: {n, of, subs, cnt}}.
- **ONE WORD, SEVERAL MEANINGS** (v172): the medium word's phrases sorted by side of the line (help/hinder/both).
- **Same-meaning declarations** (v173): `asks["same|A|B"] = "y"` rewrites A→B in the tokenizer input (`gsmSame` inside `Ht`), never in the displayed text. Asked only for two medium words where one contains the other. Split by default; merge only on the person's yes.
- **Story** (v173; opening rewritten v206): YOUR STORY SO FAR is generated prose (SY() in the Looking In builder). Since v206 the opening tells the context grasped (Q.1–Q.4 only) in order, quoting the notes: Q.1's most connected sentence, then what holds the goal back and what sits on the help side in the present, then what waits further on toward the goal (line map: x < 157 = present, y < 118 = helps; notes ranked by distance from the line, then emotion), then how help and hindrance are spread over time, and last the medium word's two jobs in one sentence. Place maps use the questions instead of position. The sections after it (What stands in the way …) draw on counts per question, emotion mix, voice, `gsmFR.it`, `gsmMED`, `gsmWS` and `gsmNR`.
- **Phrase-and-meaning layer** (v200–v201): `phBest`, `phClause`, `gsmPhTitle`, `gsmPurpose`, `gsmPhName`, `gsmSenses`, `phIn`, all wrapped in `phKeep` so the word tallies (`Mt`, `NmK`, `NmD`) don't move. No finding, card title, or theme is a lone word.
- **THE SAME THING?** (v205): shown as a prompt at the top of Pattern Analysis while unanswered (`window.gsmSameAsk`), with one sentence from the workbook for each word. Saves through `window.gsmSaveP` (a local `var P = []` near the IN THE WORDS builder hides the save function `P`; never call `P()` there).

Required behavior [D] (status):
1. **Semantic saturation filter** (✔ v172): a term found across nearly all of Q.1–Q.7 without differentiated emotional or structural context (e.g. "fire") is the medium, not a signal. Suppress it as a primary cluster node or require sub-phrase granularity.
2. **Co-occurrence neighborhood profiling** (✔ first form v172: bigram neighbors + side of the line; sliding window wider than two words still open): a sliding window tags each note's functional sub-type (fire → prescribed / unplanned / managed / mitigated / vilified / suppressed) before spatial placement or prose.
3. **Synonym families**: only by the person's declaration (✔ v173, THE SAME THING?).
4. Drop low-meaning tokens as findings ("involved", "necessary").
5. Recurrence is input to analysis, never a finding shown on its own.
6. **Lexical world-making** (optional, if clutter-free): prompt users to reframe generic terms.
7. **Perturbation audit** (optional): treat incoming notes, especially other voices, as perturbations to test against the terrain, not as instructions.

## 10. Action model (Taking Action)
Shipped v177: each way's route stats carry climb (energy), notes met by emotion, and effort / impact / benefit / return on effort (formulas in `docs/v177_update.md`). Readings per way say why to choose it; plus "Why take the harder way?", "When to stop and readjust" (Morin, 2008, p. 55) and an expandable bar chart.
Terrain module modes (one `mount`): Synthesis (default: readings, phrase loops, course model), `card` (Taking Action), `an` (Pattern Analysis: question filter + WHAT THIS SUGGESTS), `an`+`par` (In Parallel: question filter, no paragraph).

- 2–3 toggleable pathways to the same goal by different routes. Each has its rationale, how it differs, when to choose it, impact, benefit, effort (energy) and ROI, plus a comparison chart.
- **Strategy vs. program** (Morin): programs suit stable sequences; strategy is needed where the environment shifts. Actions escape their intent (ecology of action), so each pathway needs *watch signals* and a *stop-and-readjust point*.
- **Action friction field**: an action's energy cost depends on the emotional terrain it crosses.
- **Action grid: deleted** (v215) [D 11-18, his call: "I don't want the grid anywhere near this project"]. Not to be reintroduced in any form. MOVES, IN ORDER still reads each note's ease (its emotion and side of the line) from `gsmDerive`; nothing is drawn from it.
- **Synthesis→Action handoff**: a rule that turns a terrain vector (cost, height, emotional transition) into sequenced moves.
- **Reflexive loop**: logging or adjusting an action flags the upstream Q.1–Q.4 nodes and emotional contours it affects, and prompts re-synthesis.
- Constraint prompt: "What constraint or rule keeps this note in the low ground, and how do we change it?" Saves to `asks["cons|id"]`; nothing reads it yet (review 6h, Phase 2 step 8).
- Program or strategy [D 11-18]: a way from acceptance into distrust, aggression, or defiance, or one that crosses the help and hinder line, is a strategy (v202, `MixE` in `Fn`). The manual switch (`asks["mode|key"]`) wins in both directions.
- FIRST MOVES follows the way (v203, `wayMoves(k)` in the terrain module): the words follow the drawn way, not the other way round (measured; reordering the way made it far longer). Way A's zigzag is still open.
