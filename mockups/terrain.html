HOW THIS MAP IS MADE
Each note with an emotion assigned to it shapes the ground around it. More notes accumulating in one place shape it to a greater extent.
Acceptance is the highest ground. Distrust is low ground, aggression lower, and defiance the lowest: the deepest ground and the longest way back to acceptance. Indifference is a narrow ridge that climbs from the low ground up into acceptance: the way most moves between emotions go, and a place people can stay, precariously, with the ground falling away on both sides.
Where high ground meets low ground directly, a big move in either direction takes energy. Colors blend where emotions meet, more so near the line between present and goal. Broken ground (talus) marks where emotions meet. Heights are relative to your own map, not a standard measurement, as each map is unique.
An emotion is not good or bad in itself. What matters is what it is attached to and the future it produces with the others: accepting the wrong thing, or distrusting someone who has earned your trust, can lead away from the goal.
The mapping concept: Juarrero (2023) and Vigneaux (2026). Emotions are not good or bad in themselves: after Maturana and Pörksen (2011). Where it is easiest to get to: after Thompson (2007).
